# Milestone 2 — stress test

Eight comments written by Claude to sit on the boundaries of my four draft
labels, labelled by me using only the definitions. Claude didn't say which
label it would pick.

Draft definitions at the time:

- `experience`: reports what happened to the writer, with at least one checkable
  specific (a date, a round, a company, a number).
- `argued`: makes a claim about the field or gives advice and states a reason or
  mechanism for it.
- `hot_take`: makes a confident claim or gives advice with no reason offered.
- `chatter`: adds no claim and no information: questions, support, venting, jokes.

## What it showed

**Consistent:** 1, 6 and 8 all have a first-hand specific plus a claim or
choice, and all went to `experience`. So a first-hand specific wins over a claim
attached to it.

**Two places the definitions didn't decide it:**

1. `experience` vs `chatter` (4 vs 1). #4 names a company, a round and a day,
   the same kind of specific as #1, so the written definition says
   `experience`. I called it `chatter`.
2. `argued` vs `hot_take` (5 vs 2). #5 states a mechanism ("nobody's competing
   because everyone is fixated on Google"), so the written definition says
   `argued`. I called it `hot_take`. #2's reason was an observation ("every loop
   I've heard about"); #5's reason is itself just asserted.

## What I did about it

I picked `argued` vs `hot_take` as the hardest boundary and L70 (the
big-company resume screen / trading-firm OA comment) as the real post on it.
Claude made the strongest case for each label on L70 without choosing.

I then asked Claude for recommendations. It proposed the decision rule, the
tightened `experience` and `chatter` definitions, and two examples per label,
each checked against my stress-test answers (#2, #4, #5) and L70. I adopted them
as written. They're in the README's Label Taxonomy and `label_definitions.txt`.
